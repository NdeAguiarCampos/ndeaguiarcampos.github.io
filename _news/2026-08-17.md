I'm happy to share that our paper entitled ["Long-term decline in above-ground carbon sinks in Brazilian tropical and subtropical forests"](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74921-0) has just been published with open access in Nature Communications. 

This study was led by my friend [Vinícius Maia](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-5805-113X) as part of his doctoral thesis, which he finalised at the Federal University of Lavras. In it, we used a forest census database of 49 sites across a climatic gradient in eastern Brazil, totalling ~50 hectares of sampled area and 942 species of trees. 

We observed a sustained decline in the carbon sink of tropical and subtropical forests throughout the study period (1987-2020). Alarmingly, we also found that these forests are undergoing taxonomic, functional and phylogenetic homogenisation, which could have important consequences for the maintenance of essential ecosystem services provided by them.
