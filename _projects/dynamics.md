**Description:** The response of plant communities to future climate projections remains one of the major uncertainties in ecological research. Hydraulic functional traits may constitute the basis of the ecological differentiation between tropical rainforests and seasonally dry forests. Therefore, investigating the hydraulic characteristics of plants adapted to water-limited environments can provide valuable insights into the adaptations required for species resistance and persistence under increasingly arid conditions. These studies have substantially advanced our understanding of the ecology of forests across different vegetation types in eastern Brazil, including tropical rainforests, semideciduous seasonal forests, deciduous seasonal forests, and the Cerrado.

**Funding:** Brazilian Federal Agency for Support and Evaluation of Graduate Education (CAPES), National Council for Scientific and Technological Development (CNPQ), Minas Gerais State Agency for Research and Development (FAPEMIG).

I spent about four years as a research student in Federal University of Lavras's (UFLA) [LEAF Lab](https://www.lableaf.com.br/), under the supervision of Prof Rubens Santos - two years as an undergrate student, two years as a Master's student. During that time, besides working on my own projects, I was also closely involved in the research conducted by other students. The main roles I played during that time were related to:

<ul>
    <li>Field data collection</li>
        <ul>
            <li>forest plot censusing</li>
            <li>wood core collection for tree-ring analysis</li>
            <li>leaf and wood functional trait measurements</li>
        </ul>
    <li>Data analysis</li>
        <ul>
            <li>phylogenetic metrics for tree assemblages</li>
        </ul>
    <li>Scientific writing and translating</li>
        <ul>
            <li>co-writing and polishing manuscripts for publication</li>
            <li>translating manuscripts from Portuguese to English for submission</li>
        </ul>
</ul>

My time at the LEAF lab was incredibly fruitful. I was trained in all sorts of data collection, analysis and science dissemination approaches. It was an intense and rewarding period of my academic life that continues to reverberate even today, in the form of ongoing collaboration with colleagues from UFLA.
